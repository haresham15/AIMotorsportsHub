# FIA Formula 1 Sporting Regulations (2024 Condensed Summary)

This document contains a condensed version of the 2024 FIA Formula 1 Sporting Regulations for use by AI systems to accurately answer user queries regarding rules, penalties, and race procedures.

## 1. Race Weekend Format
- **Standard Weekend**: 
  - Friday: Free Practice 1 (FP1) and Free Practice 2 (FP2), each 60 minutes.
  - Saturday: Free Practice 3 (FP3) (60 minutes) followed by Qualifying.
  - Sunday: The Grand Prix (Race).
- **Sprint Weekend**:
  - Friday: FP1 (60 mins) followed by Sprint Qualifying (Sprint Shootout).
  - Saturday: The Sprint (100km race, no mandatory pit stops), followed by Grand Prix Qualifying.
  - Sunday: The Grand Prix.

## 2. Qualifying Format (Q1, Q2, Q3)
- **Q1**: 18 minutes. All 20 cars participate. The bottom 5 are eliminated (positions 16-20).
- **Q2**: 15 minutes. Remaining 15 cars. The bottom 5 are eliminated (positions 11-15).
- **Q3**: 12 minutes. Top 10 shootout for pole position.

## 3. Sprint Format & Points
- The Sprint is a race run over 100km (about one-third of a normal Grand Prix).
- Points are awarded to the top 8 finishers:
  - 1st: 8 points
  - 2nd: 7 points
  - 3rd: 6 points
  - 4th: 5 points
  - 5th: 4 points
  - 6th: 3 points
  - 7th: 2 points
  - 8th: 1 point

## 4. Points System (Grand Prix)
Points are awarded to the top 10 finishers in a standard Grand Prix:
- 1st: 25 pts, 2nd: 18, 3rd: 15, 4th: 12, 5th: 10, 6th: 8, 7th: 6, 8th: 4, 9th: 2, 10th: 1.
- **Fastest Lap**: 1 bonus point is awarded to the driver who sets the fastest lap of the race, PROVIDED they finish within the top 10.

## 5. Tire Regulations
- Supplier: Pirelli supplies all tires.
- **Dry Weather Compounds**: C1 (hardest) to C5 (softest). Pirelli selects 3 compounds per weekend (Hard, Medium, Soft).
- **Wet Weather Tires**: Intermediates (green) and Full Wets (blue).
- **Mandatory Pit Stop**: In a dry Grand Prix, every driver must use at least two different dry tire compounds, requiring at least one pit stop.

## 6. Flags & Race Control
- **Yellow Flag**: Danger ahead. Reduce speed, no overtaking. Double yellow means be prepared to stop.
- **Red Flag**: Session suspended. All cars must return to the pit lane slowly.
- **Safety Car (SC)**: Deployed for serious incidents. Cars bunch up behind the SC and cannot overtake.
- **Virtual Safety Car (VSC)**: Cars must adhere to a strict minimum lap time (delta time) and cannot overtake.
- **Blue Flag**: Warns a driver they are about to be lapped. They must let the faster car pass at the earliest opportunity.

## 7. Penalties
Common penalties issued by the stewards:
- **5-Second Time Penalty**: Served at the next pit stop or added to race time. (e.g. track limits, minor collisions).
- **10-Second Time Penalty**: Harsher version of the 5-second penalty.
- **Drive-Through Penalty**: Driver must drive through the pit lane at the speed limit without stopping.
- **Stop/Go Penalty (10 seconds)**: Driver must enter the pits, stop for 10 seconds, and leave. Mechanics cannot touch the car.
- **Grid Penalties**: Applied before the race for component changes (e.g., engine, gearbox) beyond the seasonal allocation, or severe infractions in prior sessions.

## 8. Track Limits
- Track limits are defined by the white lines at the edge of the circuit. A driver must keep at least one part of the car on or within the white lines at all times.
- Multiple violations in a race result in a black-and-white warning flag, followed by a 5-second time penalty for a fourth offense.
